# Statement and verification conditions

**Theorem (Marchal's conjecture).** The $P_{12}$ family of the planar/spatial
three-body problem connects the Lagrange equilateral triangle ($\Omega = 1$)
to the figure-eight choreography ($\Omega = 0$): there is a smooth branch of
periodic orbits parametrised by $\Omega \in [0,1]$ with the symmetries of
$P_{12}$, and the orbit at $\Omega = 0$ has the shape of a figure eight.

Reference: Calleja, García-Azpeitia, Hénot, Lessard, Mireles James,
*From the Lagrange Triangle to the Figure Eight Choreography: Proof of
Marchal's Conjecture*, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. (2026), doi:10.1090/tran/9779; arXiv:2406.17564. Section and lemma numbers below
refer to that paper, which defines every object named here.

## Setting

- Unknown: $x = (a, \beta, \alpha, u, v, w)$ with $u, v \in (\text{Fourier})^3$,
  $w \in \text{Fourier}$, all depending on $\Omega \in [0,1]$ through a Chebyshev
  expansion. Symmetry subspaces EvenCos / EvenSin / OddCos / OddSin /
  EvenFourier as in `checker/src/spaces.jl`: each is a `SymmetricSpace` of
  RadiiPolynomial, i.e. the subspace invariant under a group generated by the
  reflection $a_k = \pm a_{-k}$ and the parity $a_k = \pm(-1)^k a_k$, stored as
  one coefficient per orbit ($k \ge 0$). Products, shifts, derivatives and
  projections act on this representation; norms count every index of each orbit.
- Truncation: Fourier order $K = 70$, Chebyshev order $N = 20$.
- Banach space: $\ell^1$ with geometric weight $\nu = 1.1$ on
  Chebyshev$(N) \otimes$ Fourier$(K)$ (norm `Ell1(IdentityWeight(), GeometricWeight(ν))`).
- Map: $F(x, \Omega)$ as in Section 3 (code: `F_marchal!`), derivative
  `DF_marchal!`, both evaluated directly on the symmetric representation. The
  shifts by $2\zeta$ and $4\zeta$, $\zeta = 2\pi/3$, are $2/3$ and $4/3$ of the
  period and enter as exact roots of unity.
- Radius: $r = 10^{-6}$.
- Arithmetic: IEEE binary64 interval arithmetic with outward rounding
  (RadiiPolynomial.jl 0.11.13 on IntervalArithmetic.jl 1.0.12, correctly rounded
  elementary functions via CRlibm 1.0.2); the Chebyshev DFT, node values to
  coefficients and coefficients to the finer grids, is evaluated in 256-bit
  BigFloat intervals (`to_coef` / `to_grid` with BigFloat interval data).

## Inputs (in `data/`)

- Frozen: $\bar x$ (computing it takes a Newton continuation over the 21
  nodes). It was produced by `julia --project=search search/find_candidate.jl`
  (the `produce` command of `entry.json`), which is not part of the proof: any
  other data passing the conditions below would prove the theorem as well.
- Recomputed by the checker: $A$ (cheap, and large to store).

Details:

- $\bar x$: interval-valued node values on the 21 Chebyshev–Lobatto nodes
  $\Omega_j = \tfrac12 + \tfrac12\cos(\pi j/N)$, $j = 0,\dots,N$ (from $\Omega = 1$
  down to $\Omega = 0$). The stored interval at
  $\Omega = 1$ must enclose the exact Lagrange-triangle coefficients, the stored
  $x$-components at $\Omega = 0$ must enclose zero, and every node must be a
  real-valued function with $\beta = \alpha = 0$ (**checked by the checker, condition 0
  below**). $\bar x$ is the Chebyshev interpolant of these node values; its coefficients
  are enclosed by a rigorous DFT performed in the checker.
- $A$: on each node, $A_j$ is the floating-point inverse (LAPACK, binary64) of
  $DF(\operatorname{mid} \bar x_j, \Omega_j)$; $A$ is the Chebyshev interpolant
  of the $A_j$ (the $A_{\text{finite}}$ of the paper). The conditions do not
  depend on its exact value, only on the same $A$ being used in all of them:
  its last bits may vary with the platform and the number of BLAS threads, and
  so may the last digits of the enclosures printed for conditions 1–4.

File (raw little-endian IEEE binary64, no header), with $d$ the dimension of the
symmetric representation of $x$ ($d = 286$ for $K = 70$): `x_nodes.bin` holds, node by
node, the $d$ complex interval coefficients of $\bar x_j$ as
(inf re, sup re, inf im, sup im). Its SHA-256 digest is in `data/hashes.json`;
the checker verifies it before reading the file. Interior nodes hold the
floating-point Newton output as thin intervals.

## Conditions to verify

Each is a finite computation in outward-rounded arithmetic on the data above.
The checker prints them in this order, each prefixed `pass` or `fail`, with the
enclosure obtained.

0. **Input enclosures.**
   - 0a. Node $\Omega = 1$ encloses the Lagrange triangle.
   - 0b. Node $\Omega = 0$ encloses $U_x = V_x = 0$.
   - 0c. Every node is real-valued (cosine coefficients real, sine coefficients
     imaginary, conjugate pairs on $w$) with $\beta = \alpha = 0$, so that the
     DFT may impose the same on the interpolant.
1. **$Y$ (Lemma 3.2).** $Y := \|A F(\bar x)\|_\nu + \|\text{tail}_K F(\bar x)\|_\nu / (K+1)$
   is enclosed by a bounded interval.
2. **$Z_1$ (Lemma 3.3).**
   $Z_1 := \max\big(\|A\,DF(\bar x) - I\|_\nu + \max(\|\text{tail}(w^3\Delta u_z \Delta v_z)\|, M_1)/(K+1),\;
   \|A\| M_2 + M_1/(K+1)\big)$, with $M_1, M_2$ the explicit
   expressions of Lemma 3.3, is enclosed by a bounded interval.
3. **$Z_2$ (Lemma 3.4).** $Z_2 := \max(\|A\|, 1/(K+1)) \cdot \sup_{\|x - \bar x\| \le r} \|D^2 F(x)\|$,
   with the explicit bound on $\|D^2F\|$ of Lemma 3.4, is enclosed by a bounded interval.
4. **Radii polynomial.** $r = 10^{-6}$ lies in the interval of existence
   determined by $Y, Z_1, Z_2$ (convention of `RadiiPolynomial.interval_of_existence`;
   cf. the radii-polynomial theorem in the paper). Conclusion: a unique zero
   of $F$ in the ball of radius $r$ around $\bar x$, for every $\Omega \in [0,1]$.
5. **Figure-eight shape (Lemma 2.2 (iv)).** With
   $\mu(t) = U_y V_z - U_z V_y$ built from the validated orbit at $\Omega = 0$
   (enclosed in the ball of radius $r$):
   - 5a. $\mu(t) < 0$ for $t \in [0, 3/2]$, verified on 2000 subintervals;
   - 5b. $\mu'''(t) > 0$ for $t \in [3/2, \pi/2]$, verified on 100 subintervals.

   The
   error $\varepsilon$ of $\mu$ in $\ell^1_\nu$ is propagated to the product by
   the Banach-algebra property and to $\mu'''$ by the unweighted $\ell^1$ bound
   $\varepsilon \sup_{k} k^3 \nu^{-k} \le \varepsilon\,(3/(e \log \nu))^3$.

All conditions hold $\Rightarrow$ Theorem.
